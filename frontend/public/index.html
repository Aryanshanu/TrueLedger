The Underwriting Crisis

Financial data proves everything on its own. Nobody proves it against everything else.

In commercial credit and MSME lending, borrower information arrives across fragmented channels: banking records via NetBanking or Account Aggregator, tax returns via GSTN / GSTR-1 & 3B, and liquid investments via CAMS/KFintech. Separate analysts or point-solution tools review each stream in isolation. That structural gap is where non-performing loans and syndicated fraud take root.

Vulnerability 01
Circular Trading & Fake Invoice Networks

Shell entities cycle funds across accounts to simulate robust cashflow while GST filings reflect declining sales. In Q2 FY26, GST authorities uncovered ₹15,851 crore in fake ITC across 3,558 shell firms—a mismatch single-source algorithms approve, but cross-examination catches instantly.

Vulnerability 02
The Silent Consent Decay Liability

India's Account Aggregator network has surpassed 500 million consents enabling ₹3.82 lakh crore in lending. But consents are legally time-bound, and at that scale, nobody is tracking by hand which borrower's consent is about to lapse mid-decision - a structural gap, not an edge case.

Vulnerability 03
Manual Forensic Triangulation Friction

Reconciling months of bank deposits against GSTR-3B filings by hand is slow, repetitive cross-referencing work - the kind that scales poorly as application volume grows, and that credit-worthy commercial borrowers feel as delay.

Vulnerability 04
Black-Box Algorithmic Liability

Traditional ML scoring engines output an opaque probability score (e.g. 710 or 0.65) with zero defensible citation trail. When default strikes, internal risk committees and banking regulators cannot reconstruct the decision rationale, triggering audit penalties.
